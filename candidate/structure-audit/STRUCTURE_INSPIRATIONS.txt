 JASPERCRAFT STRUCTURES -- GAMES, TYPES AND INSPIRATIONS (CONDENSED)
===============================================================================

A NOTE ON SOURCING, UP FRONT:
This is not a transcript of your original messages. The chat turns where the
biomes and structures were actually specified predate what this session has
kept in memory (they were summarized away before this session began), and
the 234-design catalogue set was built in a separate ChatGPT Codex session
this assistant never had access to. What follows is reconstructed from what
does survive: JasperCraft's own release notes and the structure names/code
themselves, which spell most of the intent out directly (many structures are
literally internally named after their inspiration, e.g. RAPTURE, DOOM1).
No invented quotes are included.


===============================================================================
 PART 1 -- VIDEO GAMES / FILM FRANCHISES BEHIND THE WORLD
===============================================================================

SILENT HILL  (12 of the 62 regions)
  Draws on the numbered games and remake, Origins, Homecoming, Shattered
  Memories, Downpour, Book of Memories, "f", The Short Message, Townfall,
  and the handheld/arcade entries. One register structure ("The Room") also
  nods to Silent Hill 4's duplicated apartment specifically.

S.T.A.L.K.E.R.  (8 regions)
  The original trilogy, Legends of the Zone, and Heart of Chornobyl / the
  S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2 setting -- exclusion-zone, radiation-town material.

FALLOUT  (13 regions, plus individual landmarks)
  Fallout 1-4, New Vegas, 76, Tactics, Brotherhood of Steel, Shelter and
  Shelter Online. Beyond the regions themselves, two hand-built landmarks
  are direct Fallout nods: "Vault 44" (a numbered Vault) and "Bombfall"
  (a town built inside its own crater, in the mold of Fallout 3's Megaton).

DARK SOULS  (10 regions)
  All three mainline games plus their Remastered/Scholar-of-the-First-Sin
  editions and expansions.

EVIL DEAD  (10 regions)
  The 1984 game, Hail to the King, A Fistful of Boomstick, Regeneration,
  Army of Darkness: Defense, the mobile/VR entries, the 2022 game, and
  RetroRealms.

DANTE'S INFERNO  (9 regions -- all nine circles of Hell, one each)
  The 2010 action game, used circle-by-circle: Limbo, Lust, Gluttony,
  Greed, Wrath, Heresy, Violence, Fraud, Treachery.

DOOM  (3 individual landmarks, not a whole region)
  "Phobos Anomaly", "The Gate" and "Delta Labs" are literal DOOM/DOOM
  II/DOOM 3 level and location names, carried over as hand-built set pieces.

BIOSHOCK  (2 individual landmarks)
  "Rapture" (an undersea city losing to the water) and "Columbia" (a city
  meant to stay above the world, "that never got off the ground").

THE MATRIX  (1 landmark; film, not a game)
  "The Field" -- rows of pods rising past where the light reaches.

ALIEN  (1 landmark; film, not a game)
  "The Visitor" -- something landed that nobody built.

RESIDENT EVIL  (2 landmarks, by shared naming)
  "Spencer Manor" and "Raccoon Precinct" carry the same names as Resident
  Evil's Spencer Mansion and the Raccoon City Police Department.


===============================================================================
 PART 2 -- TYPES OF STRUCTURE THAT WERE BUILT
===============================================================================

HAND-BUILT SET PIECES (77 total; organized into 8 named groups)
  Landmarks    -- single recognizable buildings a town would be built around.
  Megaliths    -- large institutional-scale set pieces (bases, malls, sewers,
                  an airliner wreck).
  Wonders      -- the biggest, strangest set pieces (whole cities, a theme
                  park, a cathedral).
  Temples      -- sacred/ceremonial spaces, one per mood or element.
  Relics       -- standalone landmarks built around one strong idea each.
  Metropolis   -- downtown-scale city fragments (towers, a hotel, an
                  interchange).
  Anomalies    -- places that should not make sense; sealed self-contained
                  wrongness.
  Breach       -- small, intensely unsettling anomaly rooms.

DUNGEON ROOM TYPES (15, seeded twice as densely as everything else)
  A cell block, a round bone-vault ossuary, a flooded cistern, a sealed
  vault/strongroom, a dug-out warren, a standalone spire, a small chapel,
  a road checkpoint, a fogbound hospital ward, a ritual "trial ground", a
  woodland cabin with a hidden cellar, a shrine built around an undying
  fire, a fenced quarantine block, a dead stretch of highway, and the
  vanilla mossy-cobble spawner room (run at triple the normal frequency).

THE EXPEDITION CATALOGUE (234 designs, sorted into 36 "families" -- i.e.
kinds of building, each appearing at multiple tiers of danger and, mostly,
tied to specific horror-inspired regions):
  metro, city, metropolis, underwater_city, harbor       -- transit & urban
  house, mansion, castle, barracks, market, refuge        -- civic & domestic
  school, monastery, archive, observatory, research,
    conservatory, hospital, infirmary, bathhouse          -- institutions
  cistern, sewer, excavation, foundry, telecom, signal,
    bunker, battle_tower, prison                          -- infrastructure
    & military
  dungeon, catacomb, mortuary, reliquary                  -- death & the
                                                              sacred
  plane, backroom                                         -- wreckage and
                                                              (for "backroom")
                                                              the liminal /
                                                              Backrooms style
  auditorium                                              -- performance

LEGACY RUINS (6; disabled, never generate live -- kept only for reference)
  Small, unthemed roadside shells: a ruined town lot, a motel, a watchpost,
  a shrine, a bunker, a salvage yard. These predate the biome/catalogue
  system and are not tied to any of the inspirations above.

THE FOLD  (jaspr_backrooms)
  A separate persistent liminal world, reached through rare thresholds
  inside structures -- JasperCraft's own take on the internet "Backrooms"
  aesthetic: looping, non-spatial rooms rather than a physical place.


===============================================================================
 PART 3 -- OTHER (NON-VIDEO-GAME) INSPIRATIONS ON RECORD
===============================================================================

SCP FOUNDATION
  "Site-19" -- six cells behind glass, numbered the same way the SCP
  Foundation's own real Site-19 is.

"I HAVE NO MOUTH, AND I MUST SCREAM" (Harlan Ellison)
  The landmark "AM" -- its in-world description ("hate, and a face with no
  mouth on it") is a direct reference to the story's tormenting AI, AM.

THE SHINING
  "The Grand Meridian" -- "two hundred rooms and every door shut" reads as
  an Overlook Hotel beat.

HOUSE OF LEAVES (Mark Z. Danielewski)
  "The Walking House" -- "a house that was never one house," in the same
  vein as that novel's house that is larger on the inside than outside.

REAL-WORLD DISASTER SITES
  "Ostrovets Station" -- a cold nuclear-plant town, named after a real
  nuclear plant town in Belarus, in the Chernobyl mold already set by the
  S.T.A.L.K.E.R.-inspired regions.

CONSUMER-BRAND PARODIES (small, humor-driven landmarks, not full regions)
  "The Flatpack" (a flat-pack furniture warehouse), "The Golden Arches"
  (a derelict fast-food restaurant), "The Bell" (a derelict taco chain
  outlet) -- ordinary, recognizable chain buildings turned to ruin.
