# Owner's acceptance criteria (condensed faithfully from the owner's request, 2026-09-22)

Goal: make the existing collection coherent, complete, intentional and usable while preserving its variety
and identity. Do NOT replace structures with newly invented ones.

Owner's own words that matter most:
- "Some structures are supposed to be damaged and are ruins. They have to be all coherent. Even if they're
  ruins or not ruins, they have to be coherent. The player has to know what he's looking at."
- "I climbed up a ladder, and there was no floor that it was going to. It was just going to two floating
  spider spawners." — ladders/stairs must lead to real floors.
- "There are a lot of floating blocks. That doesn't make sense ... Floating blocks that aren't connected to
  each other need to be relocated or deleted."
- "Some structures seem a bit barren. You need to add detail as well."
- "Try to stay on theme with whatever the building is or what you think it was supposed to be. Keep guesswork
  out of the theme to a minimum. Strive for certainty and then build with the same block palette and the same
  aesthetics that it was going for. Get clues from the names or the context."
- Valuable blocks: "I don't want to encounter a structure with a million gold blocks everywhere ... replace all
  the rare earth minerals and blocks with normal blocks. If they're decorative, that's what they should be
  replaced with." (policy table in CONVENTIONS.md)

Coherency = each structure makes sense as a complete, usable place. Core acceptance requirement:
1. Complete and connected: no accidental floating blocks, disconnected fragments, missing sections, or skeletal
   buildings presented as finished. Foundations, walls, floors, roofs, pillars, balconies, decorations connect
   sensibly; upper floors/projections have believable visual support (arches/overhangs fine when their
   construction makes sense; NOT "a block under every block"). Inspect back, underside, roof, corners,
   connections between sections — a good facade does not excuse an unfinished back.
2. Appropriate interior for every building: no empty enclosed shells; not "a few random chests in an unfinished
   space". A deliberate layout with furnishings/fixtures/equipment that communicate purpose (home habitable,
   workshop supports work, warehouse organized storage + circulation). Every intended floor/room complete
   (basements, towers, upper stories, wings). Corridors/storage/open halls may be simple if the emptiness serves a
   recognizable purpose. No clutter for its own sake.
3. Layout works as a connected place: doors lead somewhere sensible; stairs/ladders connect real floors and
   usable landings; corridors connect meaningful spaces; adequate headroom. Fix doors into walls, stairs ending
   at ceilings, unreachable floors, accidental sealed rooms, obstructed entrances, balconies with no access.
   Preserve genuinely intentional secrets/obstacles.
4. Details placed logically and functioning: furniture on floors / attached to surfaces / facing sensibly; nothing
   embedded in walls, intersecting, blocking circulation or hanging unsupported. Containers, doors, work areas
   usable. Lighting (or an intentional visibility solution); deliberate darkness OK, neglectful darkness not.
5. Exterior and interior agree: windows, doors, rooflines, chimneys, floor heights and rooms correspond; no
   windows blocked by accidental walls, roofs cutting rooms, decorative entrances with no interior, exterior
   features implying nonexistent spaces. Materials/proportions/details consistent with identity. Preserve
   variety — no generic repair template.
6. Makes sense where it is placed: fix floating foundations, buried entrances, terrain intruding into rooms,
   unsupported edges, accidental overlaps. Usable approaches from surrounding terrain. Verify in natural
   generation, not only on a flat plot.
7. Intentional ruins are not an excuse: preserve genuine ruins but make surviving architecture, former layout
   and damage readable. Do not relabel unfinished as ruin, empty as minimalism, floating debris as fantasy.
   Non-buildings (bridges, gateways) need no invented rooms but must be complete and functional.
8. Verify the whole experience: "Does this make sense as a place? What is each space for? How does the player
   reach and use it? What supports and connects its parts? Does anything look accidentally missing, misplaced,
   or unfinished?"

Repair standard: preserve good structures; improve passable ones only for concrete deficiencies; repair defective
ones at the smallest sensible scope; reconstruct a section/structure only when too compromised, preserving
identity and purpose. Keep chests, spawners, entrances and gameplay features accessible and functional.
Preserve identifiers, registration, intended generation behaviour, 1.12.2 compatibility and browser performance.
Known test-world limitations that are NOT defects: catalogue chests are empty until first opened (the loot
plugin fills them); mobs do not spawn in captures.
Classification: good / passable / defective / severely incomplete — recorded separately from whether it was
actually inspected / verified. Never call something verified without having looked at its generated result.
